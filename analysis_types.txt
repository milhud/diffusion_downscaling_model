Here's a comprehensive list of all evaluation methods, metrics, and analyses used across both papers:

---

## Mardani et al. (CorrDiff) — *Communications Earth & Environment* 2025

**Deterministic Skill Metrics**
- Mean Absolute Error (MAE) — evaluated on ensemble mean and all baselines
- Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS) — using 32-member ensembles
- Pooled CRPS and MAE scores (Supplementary Table S3)
- Statistical significance testing between model differences (Supplementary Section 7.4)

**Spectral Analysis**
- Power spectra of kinetic energy (KE) at 10m
- Power spectra of 2m temperature
- Power spectra of radar reflectivity
- Wavenumber vs. energy plots to assess scale-selective variance restoration

**Distributional Analysis**
- Log-PDF plots of 10m windspeed
- Log-PDF plots of 2m temperature
- Log-PDF plots of radar reflectivity
- Tail behavior assessment (extreme value coverage)

**Calibration Analysis**
- Spread-skill ratio plots (ensemble std vs. RMSE of ensemble mean) — for all 4 output channels
- Rank histograms (Talagrand diagrams) — for all 4 channels
- Spread adjusted by factor √(1 + 1/n) per Fortin et al. (2015)

**Case Study Analyses**
- Cold front: multivariate cross-sections of temperature gradient, along-front wind, across-front wind shear; 21-line averaged NW-SE cross sections; comparison of ERA5 vs. CorrDiff vs. WRF target
- Typhoon Haikui (2023): windspeed PDF (log scale), axisymmetric radial wind profile, radius of maximum winds, ensemble spread visualization
- Extended typhoon analysis: 600 typhoon-containing time intervals 1980–2020 (Supplementary)
- Additional typhoon: Chanthu (2021) (Supplementary)
- Frontal boundary sharpness across multiple samples (Supplementary Section 7.1)

**Baselines Compared Against**
- ERA5 bilinear interpolation
- Random Forest (100 trees, per-channel, per-gridpoint)
- UNet regression (deterministic mean prediction)

**Efficiency Analysis**
- Inference time comparison vs. WRF on 928 CPU cores (×652 faster)
- Energy efficiency comparison (×1,310 more efficient) (Supplementary Section 7.3)

**Variance Analysis**
- Theoretical variance decomposition using law of total variance (Reynolds decomposition framing)
- Empirical variance reduction quantification at large scales (Supplementary Section 4)

## Lopez-Gomez et al. (R2-D2) — *PNAS* 2025

**Probabilistic Skill Metrics**
- CRPS (32-member ensembles) — primary metric, land-averaged
- MAE — deterministic limit of CRPS, for comparisons
- Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) — in Supplementary

**Distributional Fidelity**
- Quantile-quantile (Q-Q) plots at specific locations (Ensenada MX, Irvine CA, Portland OR)
- Land-averaged MAE of downscaled multimodel ensemble quantiles (0.01–0.99)
- Quantile error analysis at 99th percentile specifically for extreme risk metrics

**Spectral Analysis**
- Radially averaged energy spectra for all output fields (wind speed, relative humidity, temperature, precipitation)

**Multivariate Analysis**
- Kernel density estimates (KDE) of joint near-surface wind distributions (Shepherds Flat wind farm, Oregon)
- Tail dependence analysis of hot-and-dry compound summer extremes in southwestern US

**Bias Analysis**
- Long-term climatological bias (spatial maps) — temperature, precipitation, windspeed
- Climate change bias (absolute bias in projected change signal)
- End-of-century bias assessment (2095–2097)
- Bias reduction quantified vs. interpolation baseline

**Compound Extreme Event Analysis**
- Santa Ana Wildfire Threat Index (SAWTI) spatial maps
- Timing analysis: median day-of-year of top 5% SAWTI conditions (fall), comparing 2025–2027 vs. 2095–2097
- Generative sample spread analysis to assess coarse-resolution control of extremes (>70% of downscaling error explained)

**Uncertainty Quantification**
- Multimodel ensemble quantile fidelity assessment (8 ESMs, SSP3-7.0)
- Comparison vs. 4-member dynamical subensembles (halved ensemble size)
- Internal variability vs. model uncertainty decomposition

**Baselines Compared Against**
- Bicubic interpolation (null/zero residual baseline)
- BCSD (Bias Correction and Spatial Disaggregation)
- STAR-ESDM (5th US National Climate Assessment method)
- 4-member dynamically downscaled subensembles

**Generalization Testing**
- Cross-ESM generalization (trained on CanESM5, tested on 7 unseen ESMs)
- Climate change generalization (trained 2014–2094, tested 2095–2097)
- Sensitivity to training ESM choice (Supplementary)
- Sensitivity to training period length (Supplementary)

**Computational Efficiency**
- 97.5% cost reduction vs. full dynamical downscaling stage
- 85% total cost savings when downscaling 8 ESMs
- Throughput: >800 samples/hour on 16 NVIDIA A100 GPUs (batch size 32)
- No spin-up time required (unlike physics-based RCMs)
