# Event-based benchmarking of the latent-CorrDiff downscaler (ERA5 → CONUS404)

10 real weather events from the **test years 2018–2020** (hurricanes, a bomb cyclone, a polar-vortex outbreak,
a derecho, and two fire-weather events), each scored over a 512×512-px (~2048 km) window on the 4 km CONUS404 grid,
over the event day ±2 days. Everything here is regenerated by the scripts listed at the bottom.
Literature background (what Mardani/CorrDiff and Lopez-Gomez/R2-D2 evaluate): [`01_literature_notes.md`](01_literature_notes.md).

## TL;DR — what the events say

1. **The regression stage (DRN) does the bias/"coarse-correction" work, as in CorrDiff.** vs. bilinear ERA5 it cuts
   normalised RMSE by ~30–60 % for T2, dewpoint, winds, pressure and all derived fire-weather fields, and removes most of
   the *large-scale* (≥64 km) error (wind speed: 0.31 → 0.10 of truth variance). For T2 and wind only ~13–20 % of ERA5's MSE is a
   uniform regional offset (bias²), so the correction is mostly spatially varying, not a constant bias shift.
2. **Diffusion does not lower RMSE; it makes ensembles (CRPS) and fine-scale variance.** Ensemble-mean RMSE is slightly worse
   than the DRN, single members worse still, same pattern CorrDiff reports (MAE slightly worse than the UNet, CRPS/spectra
   better). Ensemble CRPS is 31–63 % below ERA5's MAE.
3. **Precipitation is the failure case.** The DRN is about the same as ERA5 (5 % higher RMSE), the ensemble is *worse* than ERA5 (mean +34 %,
   single member +172 % RMSE) and some members produce absurd spikes (max daily precip 3.6k–12.5k mm/day in Florence/Michael/
   Sally vs 277–457 truth; <0.03 % of pixels). Cause is very likely `expm1` of noise in log1p space (not tested).
4. **Physical consistency degrades in generated members**: 4.8 % of pixels with dewpoint > T2 (0 % truth/ERA5, 0.3 % DRN),
   4.5 % with RH > 100 %, 14.5 % negative precipitation (0.1 % DRN).
5. **Fire weather:** deterministic DRN is best at finding the top-5 % FFWI/HDW/VPD/wind pixels. The ensemble mean gains
   *moist* bias (RH |bias| 0.09σ → 0.25σ; VPD 0.07σ → 0.19σ), which would understate dry-side fire danger.
   Predicted maxima are closer to truth than ERA5's (FFWI max/truth: ERA5 0.60, DRN 0.92, ens. member 1.01).
6. **Temporal:** members are drawn independently each day. The fine-scale residual has day-to-day autocorrelation 0.05–0.10
   vs 0.23–0.52 in the truth, and day-to-day tendencies get worse than ERA5 for dewpoint/wind (see §6). This is the limitation
   Mardani et al. state; we quantify it. Only **daily** fields exist here, so sub-daily behaviour cannot be tested.
7. **Benchmark vs the two published designs (§10) — re-implementations, not the authors' weights.** The released CorrDiff/R2-D2 models
   cannot be run on CONUS events (the one public CorrDiff checkpoint I could verify, NVIDIA's *Taiwan* package, needs Taiwan-specific inputs/grid that our data lack; no CONUS weights), so I trained a
   **CorrDiff-style** (regression mean + pixel-space diffusion) and an **R2-D2-style** (pixel-space diffusion on the interpolated-coarse
   residual, no regression stage) model on our data (8 GPU-h each) and ran all three on identical events. Result: **ours and the
   CorrDiff-style model are indistinguishable on skill in this small sample (no significance test run)** (ensemble CRPS 0.221 vs 0.221 excluding precip; single-member RMSE better in 55 % of 110
   event×variable cells) and both clearly beat the R2-D2-style model (RMSE 75 %, CRPS 84 % of cells). Differences are in *character*, not accuracy:
   the pixel-space CorrDiff-style model has more fine-scale power (sometimes too much: 1.5–2.5× at <50 km) and fewer unphysical values (0.5 % vs 3.9 % dewpoint > T2), while ours
   is closer on extremes' magnitude but loses power below ~20 km. The old MRPD check (§10) stays "non-functional".

## 2. Method (and its limits)

* Model: `checkpoints/{drn,vae,diffusion}_best.pt`, 8 ensemble members, 16 Heun steps, guidance 0.2 (same as earlier plots).
* Inputs/targets: the cached **daily-mean** regridded ERA5 and CONUS404 (`/discover/nobackup/sduan/.data`).
* **Land only.** The model was trained on ≥80 %-land patches with ocean pixels filled by the patch mean, so we mimic that fill and score
  land pixels only. Hurricane cores over water are therefore excluded — results describe inland impacts, not storm intensity at sea.
* **Tiling:** DRN is blended over 3×3 overlapping 256-px tiles (removes seams; my first version used non-overlapping tiles and
  showed a visible seam — v1 metrics kept in [`v1_nonoverlap_tiling/`](v1_nonoverlap_tiling)). Diffusion residuals are drawn on
  non-overlapping 2×2 tiles (blending independent samples would artificially shrink ensemble variance), final = blended DRN + residual.
  Tile-boundary discontinuities in the *noise* remain possible.
* Derived fire-weather fields ([`src/evaluation/event_analyze.py`](../src/evaluation/event_analyze.py)): RH (Magnus, from T2 & TD2),
  VPD, wind speed, **Fosberg FFWI**, **Hot-Dry-Windy Index** (VPD × wind). All computed from **daily means**, which understate afternoon
  min-RH and peak wind — absolute values are lower than operational indices; the comparison between methods is what matters.
* "Precip" = CONUS404 `PREC_ACC_NC` (non-convective only) vs ERA5 total precipitation — a definitional mismatch that hurts every method.
* Ten events, one day each for headline numbers → small-sample; no confidence intervals were computed.
* Spectra use only the 3 windows that are >85 % land.

## 4. Bias vs. resolution — [`figures/bias_vs_resolution.png`](figures/bias_vs_resolution.png), [`figures/spectra_ratio_events.png`](figures/spectra_ratio_events.png), [`figures/bias_heatmap_events_x_variables.png`](figures/bias_heatmap_events_x_variables.png)

* RMSE falls with averaging scale for every method; the DRN/ensemble advantage over ERA5 is largest at the finest scales (4–32 km)
  for winds/T2/derived fields.
* **Surface pressure is a scale-dependent exception:** DRN RMSE 362→249 Pa from 4→128 km, ERA5 895→109 Pa. The DRN wins at fine scales but
  ERA5 interpolation is ~2× better at ≥64 km — the DRN carries a ~250 Pa large-scale pressure error (cause not investigated).
* Spectra: ERA5 loses nearly all wind power below ~100 km; DRN and members recover it (≈0.75–0.9 of truth at 10–50 km) but are low
  (≈0.3–0.5) at 200–1000 km wavelengths in this small sample. For RH, members recover fine-scale power better than the DRN
  (0.6–0.9 vs 0.2–0.6 at <100 km). Precipitation members carry >2× the truth power at large scales, the DRN <0.25× at small scales.
* Event-mean biases: ERA5 has systematic wind-speed/FFWI biases with sign depending on the event (e.g. wind ≈ −0.47σ in the two fire events,
  +0.42σ in Michael); DRN biases are within ±0.1σ in 77 % of the 110 event×variable cells (max 0.27σ) vs 46 % for ERA5 (max 0.55σ) and 64 % for the ensemble mean (max 0.47σ).

## 6. Temporal behaviour — [`figures/temporal_coherence.png`](figures/temporal_coherence.png), [`figures/temporal_aggregation.png`](figures/temporal_aggregation.png)

Only daily data → we test **day-to-day** consistency over the 5-day window, not hourly.

| metric | ERA5 | DRN | Ens mean | member |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tendency error / truth σ, T2 | 0.55 | 0.44 | 0.47 | 0.63 |
| … dewpoint | 0.52 | 0.46 | 0.56 | 0.87 |
| … wind speed | 0.68 | 0.52 | 0.58 | 0.80 |
| Per-pixel correlation with truth over the 5 days, wind speed | 0.80 | 0.85 | 0.80 | 0.70 |

* Fine-scale residual, lag-1 (1-day) autocorrelation: **member 0.05–0.10 vs truth 0.23–0.52** (T2 0.05 vs 0.52). The generated detail is
  essentially independent from day to day; the truth's is persistent (terrain/land-surface effects).
* Averaging 1 → 3 → 5 days: single-member T2 error drops 0.316 → 0.234 → 0.204 (−35 %) vs DRN 0.254 → 0.208 → 0.188 (−26 %) — consistent
  with a day-independent noise component. ERA5 wind-speed error *grows* with averaging (0.78 → 0.91): a persistent bias.
* So temporal struggles differ by component: the deterministic DRN is temporally as coherent as (or better than) ERA5 itself;
  the stochastic stage degrades it. Video/autoregressive extensions are outside this repo.

## 8. Where does the correction happen? — [`figures/bias_decomposition.png`](figures/bias_decomposition.png)

The "coarse correction vs bias correction" point: total MSE/σ² (bias² + error variance): T2 0.166 (ERA5) → 0.074 (DRN) → 0.120 (member);
wind speed 0.636 → 0.226 → 0.391. Large-scale (≥64 km) part of wind error 0.314 → 0.098 after the DRN. Diffusion adds error variance (as any
sampler does) rather than removing bias; the corrective decomposition is doing its job in the *regression* half, matching Mardani's rationale.

## 10. Benchmark against the two published designs — [`figures/baselines_*.png`](figures)

### What could and could not be done
| Model | Official weights usable here? | What I did instead |
|---|---|---|
| Mardani CorrDiff | **Not usable on CONUS.** A pre-trained CorrDiff **Taiwan** checkpoint *is* public (NVIDIA NGC "CorrDiff inference package", checkpoints Apache-2.0; `CorrDiffTaiwan` in Earth2Studio), but it is Taiwan-specific: 12 ERA5 input channels including pressure-level fields (z/t/u/v at 500 & 850 hPa, tcwv) that our surface-only cache does not hold, a 448×448 Taiwan grid, and outputs radar reflectivity + t2m/u10m/v10m. Conversely our model cannot be run on Taiwan, so there is no common test set. I could not verify any CONUS CorrDiff checkpoint (other NVIDIA variants are mentioned by third parties but were not checked). | Trained a **CorrDiff-style** model on our data: our frozen DRN (= CorrDiff's regression UNet) + a **pixel-space** EDM diffusion model on the residual `x − μ(y)`, no VAE, no CFG. |
| Lopez-Gomez R2-D2 | **No.** No weights/code; its first stage is a WRF simulation. | Trained an **R2-D2-style** model: pixel-space EDM diffusion on the residual between the fine field and the *interpolated ERA5* field (no learned regression stage). R2-D2's physics-based 45 km WRF step has no analogue here, so this is only "diffusion on a coarse-field residual". |
| MRPD (`/gpfsm/dnb33/hpmille1/model`) | Checkpoints exist but are **non-functional**: on its own training-domain crop its regression cascade has 7–13 K RMSE vs 1.9 K for plain ERA5 interpolation (stage-2 loss plateaued ≈0.51, stage-3 history empty, stage-3 checkpoints contain all-NaN `stage2_*` weights). | Not a benchmark; kept for transparency. [`figures/mrpd_T2_benchmark.png`](figures/mrpd_T2_benchmark.png) |

**These are my re-implementations**, so "CorrDiff-style"/"R2-D2-style" results say how those *designs* behave on this problem, not how NVIDIA's or Google's released models would perform.

### Fairness and limits of the comparison
* Same data, tiles (2×2 non-overlapping residual tiles, DRN/interp mean over the window), days, 8 members, 16 Heun steps, same land-only scoring, same truth.
* The baselines are **smaller (41.5 M params vs 142 M for ours)**, trained for only **8 h / 29 k steps** each with no tuning and a single seed; validation loss was still slowly falling (CorrDiff-style 0.245, R2-D2-style 0.145). The learning-rate schedule did not fully anneal (planned 38 k steps, throughput allowed 29 k). Longer training could change the ranking.
* 10 events × 1 day, no confidence intervals; "win rates" below count event×variable cells, which are not independent.
* CFG (guidance 0.2) is used by ours but not by the baselines.

### Skill — [`baselines_scorecard.png`](figures/baselines_scorecard.png), [`baselines_error_bars.png`](figures/baselines_error_bars.png), [`baselines_spread_skill.png`](figures/baselines_spread_skill.png)
RMSE / truth σ, mean over 10 events (single member unless "mean"):

| var | ERA5 | DRN | R2-D2-style | CorrDiff-style | Ours | R2-D2 mean | CorrDiff mean | Ours mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T2 | 0.38 | 0.25 | 0.38 | 0.33 | 0.32 | 0.33 | 0.28 | 0.27 |
| Wind speed | 0.78 | 0.47 | 0.76 | 0.64 | 0.62 | 0.65 | 0.50 | 0.50 |
| FFWI | 0.80 | 0.52 | 0.90 | 0.76 | 0.73 | 0.76 | 0.59 | 0.59 |
| Precip | 0.77 | 0.79 | 1.16 | 1.53 | 1.73 | 0.89 | 1.01 | 0.97 |

* **Ours vs CorrDiff-style: a tie.** Single-member RMSE lower in 55 % of the 110 cells (57 % excluding precip); CRPS lower in 45 % (44 % excl. precip). Mean CRPS/σ excluding precip: 0.221 vs 0.221. Ours is somewhat better on single-member wind/FFWI, CorrDiff-style on dewpoint/RH; ours is worst on precip single-member RMSE (1.73 vs 1.53).
* **Both beat R2-D2-style** (RMSE 75 %, CRPS 84 % of cells; CRPS 0.294 vs 0.221). The R2-D2-style single member is barely better than, and for RH/FFWI/HDW *worse than*, plain ERA5 interpolation: without a regression stage, diffusion has to learn the whole large-scale correction. This supports the "corrective/regression first" argument in the brief, with the caveat that R2-D2-style here is not the real R2-D2.
* Spread–skill (1 = calibrated): all three are under-dispersive for continuous variables (T2 0.56–0.64, winds 0.66–0.92); CorrDiff-style is slightly better calibrated on winds/FFWI (0.82–0.92 vs ours 0.77–0.86); precip: ours 1.30, R2-D2 0.96, CorrDiff 0.92; PSFC 0.2–0.3 for all.

### Fine-scale structure — [`baselines_spectra.png`](figures/baselines_spectra.png)
Single-member power relative to CONUS404 (3 land-dominated windows): CorrDiff-style **over**-shoots (T2 ≈1.5×, wind 1.5–2.5× at <50 km, RH 1.5–2.5×), R2-D2-style is near 1 for wind (1.0–1.1) but 1.2–1.5× for T2, ours is closest for T2 (≈0.9) but drops to ≈0.5× for wind and 0.4–0.6× for RH below ~20 km — plausibly the VAE's smoothing, though I did not test this. So the pixel-space models keep the finest scales but can overshoot; our latent model is the most conservative.

### Extremes, fire weather, storms — [`baselines_fire_extremes.png`](figures/baselines_fire_extremes.png), [`baselines_storm_wind.png`](figures/baselines_storm_wind.png)
* Top-5 % CSI (single member, FFWI / HDW / VPD / wind): ERA5 0.24/0.26/0.28/0.27; DRN 0.38/0.35/0.41/0.43; **R2-D2-style 0.24/0.28/0.24/0.31; CorrDiff-style 0.34/0.32/0.33/0.38; ours 0.32/0.30/0.27/0.40**.
* Predicted-max / truth-max: pixel-space members **overshoot** (FFWI 1.34 R2-D2, 1.32 CorrDiff; HDW 2.01, 1.63) while ours is 0.91–1.01. So detection is similar, magnitude calibration better for ours.
* Storm peak wind (single member, one seed, noisy): the three generative models are each within a few m/s of truth in some events and off in others (e.g. Michael truth 21.2: R2-D2 14.6, CorrDiff 23.8, ours 18.3; Laura truth 13.4: 11.2 / 17.0 / 9.5; Bomb cyclone truth 24.4: 27.9 / 29.0 / 23.3). No model is consistently best; this is too noisy for a ranking.

### Physical consistency and temporal behaviour — [`baselines_physical.png`](figures/baselines_physical.png), [`baselines_temporal.png`](figures/baselines_temporal.png)
* Pixels with TD2 > T2 (median over events, single member): CorrDiff-style **0.5 %**, R2-D2-style 1.2 %, **ours 3.9 %** (DRN alone 0.0 %); RH > 100 %: 0.4 / 1.1 / 3.7 %. Ours is the most physically inconsistent here; a mean-based statistic gives 4.8 % for ours (§9). Cause not investigated (the latent decode or the missing joint constraint are candidates).
* Precip max / truth max (median): all three spike ≈2.5–3× truth; none fixes the precip failure (§9).
* Day-to-day tendency error / truth σ (single member): T2 0.80 / 0.67 / 0.63 (R2-D2 / CorrDiff / ours), wind 0.89 / 0.81 / 0.80, dewpoint 0.91 / 0.74 / 0.87 — similar, with CorrDiff-style slightly better on dewpoint.
* Lag-1 residual autocorrelation: CorrDiff-style 0.09–0.16 and ours 0.05–0.10 (truth 0.23–0.52) — all independent-sampling models lose the persistence. The R2-D2-style value (0.27–0.38) is **not comparable**: it is computed about the interpolated-ERA5 field, whose persistent bias inflates it.

### Cost
Per 256×256 tile per member, 16 Heun steps on one A100: ours (latent, fp32, incl. DRN + VAE, CFG = 2 network calls/step) **1.10 s**; the pixel baselines (41.5 M parameters, 1 call/step) 0.67 s fp32 / 0.53 s bf16. **Against these small baselines our latent model is not faster**; the earlier "~6.8×" latent-vs-pixel timing (`results/benchmark`) compares against a full-size pixel model and does not carry over.

### Bottom line for the benchmark
On this task and budget the latent model **matches** a CorrDiff-style pixel-space model in accuracy, is better on extremes' magnitude, worse on physical consistency and finest-scale power, and no cheaper than a small pixel model. Both mean-field designs clearly beat the no-regression R2-D2-style design. A stronger claim needs the real published models (not available for CONUS), longer/larger baseline training and multiple seeds.

## 11. Caveats to keep in mind

* 10 events × 1 day; small sample, no significance testing. Event selection is by me, from memory.
* Daily means only; sub-daily/hourly resolution and diurnal extremes not assessed.
* Land-only scoring; ocean/hurricane-core behaviour not assessed. Precipitation compares different definitions.
* Ensemble size 8; no fixed seeds beyond `--seed 0` for the run; single run.
* The DRN/ensemble were evaluated on 2018–2020 (held-out test years), but no event-vs-random-day comparison was made, so I can't say whether events are
  harder than typical days.
